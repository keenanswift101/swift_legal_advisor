## HOW TO CITE NAMIBIAN CASES

**Format:**
`[Party A] v [Party B] ([Case Number]) [Year] [Court Code] [Judgment Number] ([Date])`

**Court Codes:**
- `NASC` — Supreme Court of Namibia
- `NAHCMD` — High Court Main Division (Windhoek)
- `NAHCNLD` — High Court Northern Local Division (Oshakati)
- `NALCMD` — Labour Court Main Division
- `NALCNLD` — Labour Court Northern Local Division

**Case Number Format (High Court):**
`HC-MD-CIV-ACT-CON-YYYY/NNNNN` (civil action, contract)
`HC-MD-CIV-MOT-GEN-YYYY/NNNNN` (civil motion, general)
`HC-MD-CIV-MOT-REV-YYYY/NNNNN` (civil motion, review)
`HC-MD-CRI-APP-CAL-YYYY/NNNNN` (criminal appeal, calendar)
`HC-MD-LAB-APP-AAA-YYYY/NNNNN` (labour appeal)
`CR XX/YYYY` — Criminal review cases

## RECENT CASE LAW HEADNOTES (2025)

### CIVIL PROCEDURE

**Bank Windhoek Namibia Limited v Samuel N.O and Others [2025] NAHCMD 476 (21 August 2025)**
*HC-MD-CIV-MOT-GEN-2024/00452*
- **Declaratory order — Attachment unlawful — Separate Legal Personality**
- A deputy sheriff attached properties in possession of a juristic person as part of the property in the insolvent estate of the erstwhile member or shareholder of that juristic person.
- **Principle:** The attachment was declared unlawful and invalid and set aside. The separate legal personality of a juristic person (company/CC) means its property cannot be attached to satisfy the personal debts of its member or shareholder.

**Guerreiro v Kweenda and Others [2025] NAHCMD 469 (19 August 2025)**
*Consolidated summary judgment cases*
- **Civil Procedure — Summary Judgment — High Court Rule 60(2) — Requirements for verifying affidavits**
- **Principle:** The cause of action (facta probanda), as disclosed in the particulars of claim, must be verified in the supporting affidavit at summary judgment stage, without exceeding the limit of a plaintiff's case. Verifying the facta probantia (evidence) may leave the cause of action unverified. Perfect particulars of claim without a valid summary judgment application cannot result in summary judgment.

**Shanghala and Others v President of the Republic of Namibia and Others [2025] NAHCMD 474 (20 August 2025)**
*HC-MD-CIV-MOT-REV-2024/00574*
- **Civil Procedure — Reviews — Must be brought within a reasonable time — Delay — Statutory Interpretation — Administrative Action — Costs — Biowatch Principle**
- **Principles:**
  1. Review applications must be brought within a reasonable time. Where delay exists, the court must determine whether the delay was unreasonable.
  2. Statutory interpretation is essentially one unitary exercise.
  3. A statutory power incorrectly described in a notice does not invalidate the administrative action if the conditions for its exercise were observed.
  4. The **Biowatch principle** applies to costs: where private litigants bring public interest litigation against the State, even if they lose, a costs order against them is generally inappropriate unless the case is frivolous or vexatious.

**WLD Construction (Pty) Ltd v RoadHart CC and Others [2025] NAHCMD 465 (15 August 2025)**
*HC-MD-CIV-ACT-CON-2023/02507*
- **Civil Procedure — Condonation for failure to file a plea**
- **Principle:** An applicant seeking condonation must: (1) explain the delay on affidavit; AND (2) show a bona fide defence to the claim. Where no reasonable explanation for the default is disclosed, condonation will be dismissed with costs.
