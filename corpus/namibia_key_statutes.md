## LABOUR ACT, 2007 (ACT 11 OF 2007)
**Published:** Government Gazette 3971 on 31 December 2007
**Assented:** 21 December 2007
**Latest version:** 15 March 2023 (including all amendments to 29 May 2026)
**Amendments:** Labour Amendment Act 2 of 2012; Abolition of Payment by Cheque Act 16 of 2022

### PURPOSE
To consolidate and amend labour law; establish comprehensive labour law for all employers and employees; entrench fundamental labour rights and protections; regulate basic terms and conditions of employment; ensure health, safety and welfare of employees; protect employees from unfair labour practices; regulate the registration of trade unions and employers' organisations; regulate collective labour relations; provide for systematic prevention and resolution of labour disputes; establish the Labour Advisory Council, Labour Court, Wages Commission and labour inspectorate; provide for the appointment of the Labour Commissioner and Deputy Labour Commissioner.

---

### CHAPTER 1 — INTRODUCTORY PROVISIONS

**Section 1 — Key Definitions:**

- **"Employee"**: An individual, other than an independent contractor, who works for another person and receives or is entitled to receive remuneration, or who in any manner assists in carrying on or conducting the business of an employer.
- **"Employer"**: Any person, including the State and a user enterprise, who employs or provides work for an individual and who remunerates or undertakes to remunerate that individual; or who permits an individual to assist them in carrying on their business.
- **"Remuneration"**: The total value of all payments in money or in kind made or owing to an employee arising from employment.
- **"Basic wage"**: That part of remuneration in money paid in respect of work during ordinary hours, NOT including allowances, overtime pay, additional Sunday/public holiday pay, night work additional pay, or pension/medical/insurance payments.
- **"Strike"**: A total or partial stoppage, disruption or retardation of work by employees to compel their employer or employers' organisation to accept, modify or abandon any demand that may form the subject matter of a dispute of interest.
- **"Lockout"**: A total or partial refusal by one or more employers to allow their employees to work, to compel employees to accept, modify or abandon any demand.
- **"Collective agreement"**: A written agreement concerning terms and conditions of employment or any other matter of mutual interest, concluded by one or more registered trade unions on the one hand and one or more employers/employers' organisations on the other.
- **"Essential service"**: A service the interruption of which would endanger the life, personal safety or health of the whole or any part of the population of Namibia.
- **"Trade union"**: An association of employees whose principal purpose is to regulate relations between employees and their employers.
- **"Dispute of interest"**: Any dispute concerning a proposal for new or changed conditions of employment (not required to be resolved by adjudication or arbitration).
- **"Independent contractor"**: A self-employed individual who works for or renders services to a user enterprise or customer as part of that individual's business, undertaking or professional practice.
- **"Spouse"**: A partner in a civil marriage or a customary law union or other union recognised as a marriage in terms of any religion or custom.

**Section 2 — Application of Act:**
- Section 5 (anti-discrimination) applies to ALL employers and employees.
- All other sections apply to all employers and employees EXCEPT members of the Namibian Defence Force, Namibian Police Force, Namibia Central Intelligence Service, and Prison/Correctional Service (unless their specific Acts provide otherwise).
- If there is a conflict between the Labour Act and another law governing the same employees, the law that is more favourable to the employee prevails.

### CHAPTER 2 — FUNDAMENTAL RIGHTS AND PROTECTIONS

**Section 3 — Child Labour:**
- Absolute prohibition on employing any child under **14 years**.
- Children aged **14–15**: Cannot work underground/in mines, in construction/demolition, manufacturing, electricity generation, machinery installation, or any activity endangering health/safety/moral/social development. Cannot work between **20h00 and 07h00**.
- Children aged **16–17**: Cannot work between **20h00 and 07h00** or in dangerous work (unless Minister permits by regulation).
- **Penalty**: Fine not exceeding N$20,000 or imprisonment up to 4 years or both.

**Section 4 — Forced Labour:**
- No person may directly or indirectly cause, permit or require any individual to perform forced labour.
- Forced labour includes: work performed involuntarily under threat of penalty; work performed by an employee's child under 18 in terms of an employer-employee arrangement; work performed because an individual is under the control/supervision of a traditional leader.
- **Penalty**: Fine not exceeding N$20,000 or imprisonment up to 4 years or both.

**Section 5 — Prohibition of Discrimination and Sexual Harassment:**
Prohibited grounds of discrimination in any employment decision:
- Race, colour, or ethnic origin
- Sex, marital status or family responsibilities
- Religion, creed or political opinion
- Social or economic status
- Degree of physical or mental disability
- AIDS or HIV status
- Previous, current or future pregnancy

Affirmative action measures for racially disadvantaged persons, women, or persons with disabilities are NOT discrimination. Selecting persons on reasonable criteria of ability, capacity, productivity and conduct is NOT discrimination.

**Sexual harassment** = unwarranted conduct of a sexual nature that constitutes a barrier to equality in employment. Where an employee resigns as a result of sexual harassment by an employer, the resignation constitutes **constructive dismissal**, which may constitute **unfair dismissal**.

**Section 6 — Freedom of Association:**
No person may prejudice an employee or job-seeker because of past, present or anticipated: exercise of rights under the Act or contract; disclosure of information they are entitled to give; failure/refusal to do something unlawful; membership of a trade union; or participation in lawful trade union activities.

### CHAPTER 3 — BASIC CONDITIONS OF EMPLOYMENT

**Section 16 — Ordinary Hours of Work:**
- Maximum **45 hours per week**; not more than **9 hours per day** (5-day work week) or **8 hours per day** (6-day work week).
- Security officers/emergency healthcare/designated classes: Maximum **60 hours per week**; not more than **12 hours per day** (5-day week) or **10 hours per day** (6-day week).

**Section 17 — Overtime:**
- Only by agreement; not more than **10 hours per week** and not more than **3 hours per day**.
- Overtime rate: At least **1.5 times** the employee's hourly basic wage.
- Overtime on Sundays/public holidays (for employees who ordinarily work those days): At least **double** the hourly basic wage.

**Section 18 — Meal Intervals:**
- Employee working more than **5 consecutive hours**: At least **1 hour** meal interval (or minimum **30 minutes** by agreement with written notice to Permanent Secretary).
- Employee to be remunerated for meal interval longer than **90 minutes**.

**Section 19 — Night Work:**
- Additional payment of **6% of hourly basic wage** for each hour worked between **20h00 and 07h00**.
- Pregnant employees must not work between 20h00 and 07h00 for **8 weeks before** expected confinement and **8 weeks after** confinement (extendable by medical certificate).

**Section 20 — Rest Periods:**
- Maximum spread-over (start to finish): **12 hours**.
- Minimum weekly rest period: **36 consecutive hours**.

**Section 21 — Sunday Work:**
- Double the employee's hourly basic wage for each hour worked on a Sunday (or 1.5 times + equal time off the next week by agreement).
- For employees who ordinarily work on Sundays: Normal daily remuneration + hourly basic wage per hour worked.

**Section 22 — Public Holidays:**
- Employee who does not work on a public holiday (that falls on their ordinary working day): Must be paid normal daily remuneration.
- Employee who works on a public holiday: Normal daily remuneration + hourly basic wage per hour worked (or 1.5 times basic wage per hour + equal time off next week by agreement).

**Section 23 — Annual Leave:**
- Entitlement: **4 consecutive weeks' annual leave** per annual leave cycle.
  - 6-day week: 24 working days
  - 5-day week: 20 working days
  - 4-day week: 16 working days
  - 3-day week: 12 working days
- Must be taken not later than **4 months** after end of annual leave cycle (or **6 months** by written agreement).
- Cannot be substituted for cash payment except on termination of employment.

**Section 24 — Sick Leave:**
- Sick leave cycle: **36 consecutive months**.
- Entitlement during sick leave cycle: **30 working days** (5-day week) or **36 working days** (6-day week).
- During first year of employment: **1 day per 26 days worked**.
- No obligation to pay after **2 consecutive days of absence** without a medical certificate.

**Section 25 — Compassionate Leave:**
- **5 working days** per 12-month period.
- Entitlement upon death or serious illness in the family (child, spouse, parent, grandparent, sibling, in-laws).

**Section 26 — Maternity Leave:**
- Minimum **12 weeks' maternity leave** after 6 months' continuous service.
- Commences **4 weeks before** expected date of confinement; **8 weeks after** confinement.
- During maternity leave: Contract of employment remains in force; employer pays all remuneration EXCEPT basic wage; Social Security Commission pays a portion of basic wage.
- Employer must NOT dismiss an employee during or at expiry of maternity leave on grounds related to pregnancy, delivery, or family status/responsibility.

**Section 30 — Notice for Termination of Employment:**
- Employed ≤ 4 weeks: **1 day** notice
- Employed > 4 weeks but ≤ 1 year: **1 week** notice
- Employed > 1 year: **1 month** notice
- Notice must be in writing with reasons (for employer-initiated termination) and date.
- An illiterate employee may give oral notice.
- Notice may not be given during annual, sick, compassionate, or maternity leave.

**Section 31 — Payment Instead of Notice:**
An employer may pay the employee the remuneration they would have received during the notice period, instead of giving notice.

**Section 33 — Unfair Dismissal:**
An employer must not dismiss an employee without a valid and fair reason AND without following a fair procedure. It is AUTOMATICALLY UNFAIR to dismiss an employee because they: disclosed information they are entitled to; refused to do something unlawful; exercised a right under the Act; belong to/participated in a trade union. It is unfair to dismiss on grounds of sex, race, colour, ethnic origin, religion, creed, social/economic status, political opinion or marital status.

**PRESUMPTION**: Once the employee establishes the existence of dismissal, it is presumed (unless the employer proves otherwise) that the dismissal is unfair.

**Section 34 — Redundancy/Collective Dismissal:**
If dismissal is for reduction of workforce for economic/technological reasons:
1. Employer must inform Labour Commissioner and exclusive bargaining agent at least **4 weeks before** intended dismissals.
2. Must disclose all relevant information.
3. Must negotiate in good faith on alternatives, selection criteria, minimising dismissals.
4. Must select employees according to agreed or fair and objective criteria.

**Section 35 — Severance Pay:**
- Employees with **12+ months of continuous service** are entitled to severance pay on dismissal, death in employment, or resignation/retirement at age 65.
- Rate: At least **1 week's remuneration per year of continuous service**.
- NOT payable on fair dismissal for misconduct or poor work performance, or unreasonable refusal of reinstatement.

**Section 37 — Certificates of Employment:**
On termination, employer must provide a certificate of service stating: full name; employer name and address; industry; commencement and termination dates; job description; remuneration at termination; and (if requested) reason for termination.

### CHAPTER 4 — HEALTH, SAFETY AND WELFARE

**Section 39 — Employer Duties:**
Must, free of charge: provide a safe working environment without risk to health; provide and maintain safe plant, machinery and work systems; provide safe entry and exit; provide adequate personal protective clothing and equipment; provide necessary information and training; ensure safe use, handling, storage and transport of substances; ensure adequate organisation of work hours.

**Section 41 — Employee Duties:**
Must: take reasonable care for own and others' safety and health; co-operate with employer to fulfil the employer's duties under this Chapter.

**Section 42 — Right to Leave Dangerous Workplace:**
If an employee has reasonable cause to believe it is not safe to continue work, they may leave. They retain the same conditions of service and remuneration during absence.

**Section 43 — Health and Safety Representatives:**
- Workplaces with **11–100 employees**: At least 1 health and safety representative.
- Workplaces with **101+ employees**: At least 1 per first 100 employees; 1 additional per each additional 100.
- Term: **2 years**, eligible for re-election.
- Employer must grant time off during working hours without loss of pay.

**Section 46 — Health and Safety Committees:**
Required in workplaces with **more than 100 employees** (upon request of a health and safety representative). Consists of all health and safety representatives + equal number of employer representatives.

---

### CHAPTER 5 — UNFAIR LABOUR PRACTICES
- Unfair disciplinary action (not just dismissal) is prohibited.
- Trade union unfair labour practices: refusing to bargain collectively; bad faith bargaining; intimidation; failure to fairly represent members.
- Employer unfair labour practices: refusing to bargain collectively; bad faith bargaining; failing to disclose relevant information; unilaterally altering terms and conditions; seeking to control a trade union; intimidation.

---

### CHAPTER 6 — TRADE UNIONS AND EMPLOYERS' ORGANISATIONS

**Section 57 — Registration Requirements:**
A trade union or employers' organisation that adopts a constitution complying with section 53 may apply to the Labour Commissioner for registration.

**Section 58 — Effect of Registration:**
A registered trade union or employers' organisation is a **juristic person**. Members, office-bearers, and officials are not personally liable for the organisation's liabilities.

**Section 64 — Recognition as Exclusive Bargaining Agent:**
A registered trade union representing the **majority** of employees in an appropriate bargaining unit is entitled to recognition as exclusive bargaining agent for the purpose of negotiating collective agreements.

**Section 70 — Legal Effect of Collective Agreements:**
A collective agreement binds: the parties; members of registered trade unions that are parties; members of registered employers' organisations that are parties; all employees in the recognised bargaining unit (if the trade union has been recognised as exclusive bargaining agent); and any persons to whom the agreement has been extended.

---

### CHAPTER 7 — STRIKES AND LOCKOUTS

**Section 74 — Right to Strike or Lockout:**
Requirements before a lawful strike/lockout:
1. Dispute referred to Labour Commissioner for conciliation.
2. Party attended conciliation meetings.
3. Dispute remains unresolved after **30 days** from date of referral.
4. **48 hours' notice** given to Labour Commissioner and other parties.
5. Strike/lockout conforms to agreed or conciliator-determined rules.

**Section 75 — Prohibited Strikes/Lockouts:**
Cannot strike/lockout if: statutory requirements not complied with; dispute must be arbitrated; parties agreed to arbitrate; dispute governed by arbitration award or court order; parties engaged in a designated essential service.

**Section 76 — Protected Strikes:**
Taking part in a lawful strike or lockout does NOT constitute a delict or breach of contract. Employees may hold a peaceful picket near the place of employment. Employer must not require non-striking employees to do striking employees' work (except to prevent danger to life/health) and must not hire replacement workers.

**Section 77 — Essential Services:**
Designated by Minister on recommendation of the Labour Advisory Council (following Essential Services Committee investigation and public hearing process). Parties in essential services cannot strike or lockout; disputes must be referred to arbitration.

---

### CHAPTER 8 — DISPUTE PREVENTION AND RESOLUTION

**Section 82 — Conciliation:**
- Any party to a dispute may refer it to the Labour Commissioner (or any labour office).
- The Labour Commissioner designates a conciliator.
- Conciliation must be attempted within **30 days** of referral (or longer by agreement).
- Parties may be represented by trade union/employer org officials, co-employees, or (by permission) legal practitioners.
- If unresolved, conciliator issues certificate of unresolved dispute.

**Section 85 — Arbitration Tribunals:**
Arbitration tribunals operate under the auspices of the Labour Commissioner. Have jurisdiction to hear and determine any dispute arising from the interpretation, implementation or application of the Labour Act. Arbitrators must be independent and impartial.

**Section 86 — Referral to Arbitration:**
Time limits for referral:
- Dismissal disputes: Within **6 months** after dismissal.
- All other disputes: Within **1 year** after the dispute arises.
Arbitrator may make any appropriate award including: interdict; order directing performance; declaratory order; reinstatement of employee; compensation; costs (only if frivolous/vexatious conduct).
Award must be issued within **30 days** of conclusion of proceedings.

**Section 87 — Effect of Arbitration Awards:**
A binding arbitration award becomes an order of the Labour Court upon filing by any party or the Labour Commissioner.

**Section 89 — Appeals from Arbitration:**
Party may appeal to Labour Court on question of law (within **30 days** after award served). Review available for gross irregularity, misconduct, or award improperly obtained (within **30 days**, or 6 weeks if corruption involved).

---

### CHAPTER 9 — LABOUR INSTITUTIONS

**Labour Commissioner:**
Appointed by the Minister. Functions: receive and process disputes; attempt conciliation; arbitrate unresolved disputes; compile and publish statistics.

**Labour Advisory Council:**
Continues from previous Act. Consists of: Chairperson + 4 State representatives + 4 trade union representatives + 4 employers' organisation representatives. Advises Minister on all labour policy matters.

**Wages Commission:**
Investigates terms and conditions of employment and remuneration. Reports to Minister with recommendations for wage orders.

**Labour Court:**
A division of the High Court. Judge-President assigns suitable High Court judges. Exclusive jurisdiction over: appeals from Labour Commissioner decisions; reviews of arbitration awards; review of Ministerial/official decisions under the Act; declaratory orders; urgent relief/interdicts. Costs order against a party only if that party acted in a frivolous or vexatious manner.

---

### CHAPTER 10 — GENERAL PROVISIONS

**Section 128 — Private Employment Agencies:**
An individual placed by a private employment agency with a user enterprise is an **employee of the user enterprise** (the user enterprise is the employer), unless an exemption is granted by the Minister.

**Section 128A — Presumption of Employee Status:**
An individual who works for or renders services to any other person is **presumed to be an employee** if any one of the following factors is present:
- Manner of work is subject to control/direction of the other person
- Hours of work are subject to control/direction
- Work forms an integral part of the organisation
- Average of at least 20 hours per month over past 3 months
- Economically dependent on the other person
- Provided with tools/equipment by the other person
- Only works for that one person

**Section 128C — Presumption of Indefinite Employment:**
An employee is presumed to be employed indefinitely, unless the employer can establish a justification for fixed-term employment. (Does not apply to managerial employees.)

**Section 130 — Employment Records:**
Every employer must keep a record for the most recent **5 years** containing: employee name, sex, age, occupation; commencement date; termination date and reasons; remuneration details; periods of absence. Records must be retained for **5 years after termination** of the relevant employee.

## ANTI-CORRUPTION ACT, 2003 (ACT 8 OF 2003)
**Published:** Government Gazette 3037 on 4 August 2003
**Commenced:** 15 April 2005
**Latest version:** 25 October 2016 (amended by Anti-Corruption Amendment Act 10 of 2016)

### PURPOSE
To establish the Anti-Corruption Commission and provide for its functions; to provide for the prevention and punishment of corruption; and to make provision for matters connected therewith.

---

### CHAPTER 1 — PRELIMINARY

**Key Definitions:**
- **"Corrupt practice"**: Any conduct in Chapter 4 of the Act.
- **"Gratification"** (very broad): Includes money, gifts, loans, fees, rewards, commissions, property, office/dignity/employment, payment/release/discharge of any obligation, any valuable consideration or benefit, any service or favour, any right or privilege, any aid/vote/consent/influence, any offer or promise of any of the above.
- **"Agent"**: A person employed by or acting for another in any capacity including trustees, assignees, liquidators, executors, legal representatives, public officers, trustees, administrators, subcontractors.
- **"Principal"**: Includes any employer, trust beneficiary, estate beneficiary, public body.
- **"Public body"**: Any ministry/office/agency of government; any regional council or local authority council; any corporation, board, council, institution or other body exercising public power or performing public functions.
- **"Public officer"**: Any member, officer, employee, or servant of a public body including staff members of public service (police, prison, defence), National Assembly/National Council/regional council/local authority members, Judges, and any person receiving remuneration from public funds.

---

### CHAPTER 2 — THE ANTI-CORRUPTION COMMISSION

**Section 2 — Establishment:**
An independent and impartial body known as the **Anti-Corruption Commission** (ACC). Consists of: Director-General; Deputy Director-General; other staff members. It is an agency in the Public Service.

**Section 3 — Functions of the ACC:**
- Receive or initiate and investigate allegations of corrupt practices
- Consider whether investigation is needed and if so, whether ACC should conduct it or refer to another authority
- Consult, co-operate and exchange information with appropriate bodies, including foreign bodies
- Assemble and furnish evidence to prosecuting or other authorities
- Investigate conduct of persons employed by public/private bodies which may be connected with corruption
- Take measures for prevention of corruption in public and private bodies (examining practices, advising, educating, enlisting public support)
- Disseminate information about the ACC's functions

**Section 4 — Director-General and Deputy Director-General:**
Appointed by the **National Assembly** upon nomination by the **President**. Must be of good character and high integrity with relevant knowledge or experience. Serve for **5 years**, reappointable. Full-time; cannot engage in day-to-day management of any business, take part in management of any political party, or be a salaried employee of any person or organisation.

**Disqualifications (section 5):** Non-Namibian citizens; National Assembly/Council members; regional council/local authority council members; unrehabilitated insolvents; persons convicted of dishonesty offences or imprisonment without option of a fine.

**Removal (section 9):** President notifies Chief Justice who appoints a board (including at least one former or qualified Judge) to investigate. National Assembly must adopt a resolution calling for removal.

---

### CHAPTER 3 — INVESTIGATION OF CORRUPT PRACTICES

**Section 17 — Reporting Corrupt Practices:**
Any person may furnish information to the ACC about any matter they suspect on reasonable grounds concerns a corrupt practice. Information may be given orally or in writing.

**Section 18 — Receipt and Examination of Allegations:**
ACC must receive allegations and examine each to decide if investigation is warranted. Considerations include: seriousness of conduct; whether allegation is frivolous or vexatious; whether conduct is already being investigated by another authority; whether investigation is justified and in the public interest.

**Section 21 — Investigation:**
Person questioned by an authorised officer must answer truthfully and to the best of their ability but is NOT obliged to answer any self-incriminating question. Self-incriminating answers/statements are inadmissible in criminal proceedings except for perjury or offences under section 29 (obstruction, etc.).

**Section 22 — Entry and Search with Warrant:**
Authorised officer may enter premises under a warrant issued by a **High Court Judge** or **Magistrate**, supported by affidavit. Warrant valid until executed, cancelled, purpose lapses, or **1 month** after issue. Execution between **06h00 and 18h00** (unless judge/magistrate authorises otherwise).

**Section 23 — Entry and Search Without Warrant:**
May enter premises (other than private dwelling) without a warrant if: occupier consents; or reasonable grounds that a warrant would be issued and that delay would defeat the purpose.

**Section 25 — Conduct of Entry and Search:**
Must be conducted with strict regard for decency, order, and each person's right to dignity, freedom, security and privacy. Search of a person may only be conducted by a person of the **same gender**. Before questioning, person must be advised of right to be assisted by a legal practitioner.

**Section 26 — Power to Obtain Asset Information:**
Director-General may, by written notice, require: a suspected person to provide a statement of all property/assets; another person with whom the suspect had financial dealings to provide information; any person to provide relevant documents; banks/financial institutions to provide account statements — notwithstanding any law on secrecy.

**Section 28 — Powers of Arrest:**
Authorised officer may arrest without a warrant any person reasonably suspected of having committed or being about to commit an offence under the Act. Arrested person must be taken to a police station to be dealt with under the **Criminal Procedure Act 51 of 1977**.

**Section 31 — Referral to Prosecutor-General:**
Upon completion of investigation, if evidence of corruption found, Director-General MUST refer the matter to the **Prosecutor-General**. If the PG decides to prosecute, they may delegate authority to conduct proceedings to suitably qualified ACC staff.

---

### CHAPTER 4 — CORRUPT PRACTICES AND PENALTIES

**Section 33 — Offence of Corruptly Accepting Gratification:**
It is an offence to directly or indirectly corruptly solicit, accept or agree to accept any gratification as an inducement to do/omit anything, or as a reward for having done/omitted anything.

**Section 34 — Offence of Corruptly Giving Gratification:**
It is an offence to directly or indirectly corruptly offer, give or agree to give any gratification as an inducement or reward for doing/omitting anything.

**Section 35 — Corruption by or of Agent:**
Both the agent (who solicits/accepts) and the person who offers/gives gratification in relation to the principal's affairs commit offences. It is an offence to knowingly use false documents to mislead a principal.

**Section 36 — Corrupt Acquisition of Private Interest by Public Officer:**
A public officer commits an offence if they knowingly and corruptly acquire or hold a private interest in any contract, agreement or investment connected with the public body they serve.

**Section 37 — Corruption in Relation to Tenders:**
Offering/accepting gratification for the withdrawal of or refraining from making a tender is an offence.

**Section 38 — Bribery of Public Officer:**
Offering/giving/accepting gratification to/from a public officer in relation to: voting at public body meetings; performing/omitting official acts; procuring/preventing votes/contracts; showing favour or disfavour — all constitute offences.

**Section 43 — Corruptly Using Office or Position:**
A public officer commits an offence if they corruptly use their office/position to obtain any gratification. PROOF that a public officer made a decision in a matter in which they or a relative/associate had an interest is, in the absence of evidence to the contrary, SUFFICIENT EVIDENCE of the offence. "Relative" includes spouses, fiancés, children (including step and foster children), parents (including step and foster), and siblings.

**Section 48 — Duty to Report:**
Public officer who receives an offer of gratification MUST report to supervisor and to ACC as soon as possible. Person from whom gratification is demanded/solicited MUST report to ACC. Failure to report is an offence.

**Section 49 — Penalties:**
Fine not exceeding **N$500,000** or imprisonment not exceeding **25 years**, or both.

**Section 50 — Extraterritorial Jurisdiction:**
The Act applies to Namibian citizens and persons domiciled/permanently resident in Namibia even for offences committed outside Namibia.

**Section 51 — Magistrates' Courts Jurisdiction:**
Any magistrate's court has jurisdiction to impose any penalty under the Act.

**Section 52 — Protection of Informers:**
Witnesses not obliged to disclose identity or address of informers. Court may only require disclosure if informer made a material false statement or if justice cannot be done without disclosure. No disciplinary, civil or criminal proceedings may be instituted against an informer for information provided in good faith to the ACC.

## HIGH COURT ACT, 1990 (ACT 16 OF 1990)
**Published:** Government Gazette 85 on 8 October 1990
**Commenced:** 8 October 1990
**Latest version:** 22 August 2025 (amended by High Court Amendment Act 2 of 2024 and Dissolution of Marriages Act 10 of 2024)

### PURPOSE
To make provision for the jurisdiction of the High Court of Namibia in pursuance of Article 80 of the Namibian Constitution; the making of rules of court; and to provide for matters incidental thereto.

---

**Section 1 — Definitions:**
- **"High Court"**: The High Court of Namibia constituted under Article 80(1) of the Namibian Constitution.
- **"Full court"**: A court consisting of more than two judges.
- **"Registrar"**: The registrar of the High Court (includes assistant registrar).
- **"Sheriff"** / **"Deputy-sheriff"**: Officers appointed under section 30.
- **"Primary home"**: A dwelling which a person uses as their main place of residence.

**Section 2 — General Jurisdiction:**
The High Court has jurisdiction to hear and determine all matters conferred or imposed upon it by the Act, the Namibian Constitution, or any other law.

**Section 2A — Divisions:**
The High Court consists of: the **main division** (Windhoek); and **local divisions** established by Presidential Proclamation. Process or orders of either division are of force throughout Namibia. Local divisions have concurrent jurisdiction with the main division in their area. (Current divisions: Main Division — Windhoek; Northern Local Division — Oshakati.)

**Section 3 — Qualifications for Appointment as Judges:**
Must have: held office as a judge of a superior court in a Commonwealth country; or held office as a judge in a country with a comparable legal system; or practised as a legal practitioner in Namibia or qualifying country for **at least 5 years**; or served in a legal capacity in full-time public service for a cumulative **5 years**; or be a (non-additional) magistrate with a law degree and **5 years** presiding experience.

**Section 10 — Constitution of a Court:**
- Civil matters: Single judge (unless Judge-President directs full court).
- Criminal cases: As prescribed by applicable criminal procedure law.
- During vacation or when only one judge available: One judge may exercise all the powers of the High Court.

**Section 13 — Open Court:**
All proceedings in the High Court shall be carried on in open court (subject to Article 12(1)(a) and (b) of the Constitution — press/public may be excluded for morals, public order, national security, or juvenile interests).

**Section 16 — Jurisdiction:**
High Court has jurisdiction over all persons residing or being in Namibia and in relation to all causes arising and all offences triable within Namibia. Specifically: hear and determine appeals from all lower courts; review proceedings of all lower courts; grant declaratory orders on existing, future or contingent rights.

**Section 18 — Appeals in Civil Proceedings:**
Appeals from High Court (as court of first instance): To the **Supreme Court, as of right** (no leave required). Appeals from High Court (as court of appeal): To the Supreme Court only with **leave** of the High Court or, if refused, the Supreme Court.

**Section 20 — Grounds for Review of Lower Court Proceedings:**
- Absence of jurisdiction
- Interest in the cause, bias, malice or corruption of the presiding officer
- Gross irregularity in the proceedings
- Admission of inadmissible evidence or rejection of admissible evidence

**Section 25 — Prohibition on Attachment to Found Jurisdiction:**
No attachment of person or property to found jurisdiction shall be ordered against any person residing in Namibia.

**Section 35 — Property Not Liable to Seizure in Execution:**
Exempt from execution: necessary beds, bedding and wearing apparel; necessary furniture and household utensils up to R1,500 in value; livestock, tools and agricultural implements up to R1,500; food sufficient for one month; tools and implements of trade up to R1,500; professional books/documents/instruments up to R1,500; legally required arms and ammunition.

**Section 35A — Restriction on Sale in Execution of Immovable Property:**
No sale in execution of immovable property unless authorised by court order following a process where: the court is satisfied that the judgment debtor has insufficient movable property; and the court has declared the property executable. If the property is the **primary home**, the court must hold an inquiry to satisfy itself that the sale is the most appropriate order — if not, alternative orders may be made (e.g., attaching other property, varying repayment, allowing voluntary sale, etc.).

**Section 36 — Offences Relating to Execution:**
Obstructing/hindering a sheriff; refusing to identify oneself; disposing of goods under attachment; falsely declaring no property to satisfy a warrant; refusing to point out property — all are offences. Penalty: Fine not exceeding R1,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 6 months or both.

**Section 39 — Rules of Court:**
The Judge-President may make rules regulating: conduct of proceedings; service and execution of process; appointment of commissioners to take evidence; tariff of court fees; fees for legal practitioners; taxation of bills of costs; execution of judgments (including against primary home); compulsory alternative dispute resolution; judicial case management (including appointment of court experts, electronic filing); appointment and conduct of deputy-sheriffs.
