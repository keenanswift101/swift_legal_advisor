## 1. LEGAL SYSTEM OVERVIEW

Namibia is a **sovereign, secular, democratic and unitary State** founded upon the principles of democracy, the rule of law and justice for all (Article 1 of the Namibian Constitution).

**Independence:** 21 March 1990  
**Capital (seat of government):** Windhoek  
**Official language:** English  
**Legal tradition:** Mixed — Roman-Dutch common law (inherited from South African administration), customary law, and constitutional law since 1990.

### Sources of Law (in order of hierarchy):
1. **The Namibian Constitution** — Supreme Law of Namibia (Article 1(6))
2. **Acts of Parliament** (legislation passed by the National Assembly and National Council)
3. **Subsidiary Legislation** (regulations, notices made under Acts)
4. **Common Law** (Roman-Dutch and English common law, as received)
5. **Customary Law** (recognised where not inconsistent with the Constitution or statute)
6. **International Law** — General rules of public international law and binding international agreements form part of Namibian law (Article 144)

### Constitutional Supremacy:
> "This Constitution shall be the Supreme Law of Namibia." — Article 1(6)

Any law or action inconsistent with the Constitution is invalid to the extent of the inconsistency (Article 25(1)).

## 2. COURT STRUCTURE & HIERARCHY

### 2.1 Supreme Court of Namibia
- **Highest court** in Namibia
- Hears appeals from the High Court
- Handles constitutional matters and matters referred by the Attorney-General
- Presided over by the **Chief Justice**
- Quorum: **3 Judges**
- Decisions are **binding on all other courts** in Namibia (Article 81)

### 2.2 High Court of Namibia
**Divisions:**
- **High Court Main Division** (Windhoek) — NAHCMD
- **Northern Local Division** (Oshakati) — NAHCNLD

- Has **original jurisdiction** over all civil disputes and criminal prosecutions
- Hears appeals from Magistrates' Courts and Labour Courts
- Presided over by the **Judge-President**
- The Judge-President is also the **Deputy-Chief Justice** and ex officio Judge of the Supreme Court

### 2.3 Labour Court
- **Labour Court Main Division** — NALCMD
- **Labour Court, Northern Local Division**
- Specialised court for employment and labour disputes
- Established under the Labour Act 11 of 2007

### 2.4 Electoral Court
- Specialised court for electoral disputes
- Established under the Electoral Act 5 of 2014

### 2.5 Magistrates' Courts (Lower Courts)
- Established under the Magistrates' Courts Act 32 of 1944
- Presided over by Magistrates
- Regulated by the **Magistrates Commission** (established under the Magistrates Act 3 of 2003)
- Handles most criminal and civil matters at first instance

### 2.6 Community Courts
- Established under the Community Courts Act 10 of 2003
- Apply customary law
- Jurisdiction limited to customary law disputes within traditional communities

### 2.7 Key Officers of the Court:
- **Chief Justice** — Head of the judiciary, supervises all courts
- **Attorney-General** — Principal legal adviser to the President and Government; final responsibility for the office of the Prosecutor-General
- **Prosecutor-General** — Prosecutes in the name of the Republic of Namibia; conducts criminal prosecutions
- **Ombudsman** — Independent constitutional body; investigates violations of fundamental rights, abuse of power, and maladministration
- **Auditor-General** — Audits the State Revenue Fund

## 3. THE NAMIBIAN CONSTITUTION (FULL TEXT)

**Published:** Government Gazette No. 2, 21 March 1990  
**Commenced:** 21 March 1990 (date of Independence)  
**Amendments:**
- First Amendment Act 34 of 1998 (24 December 1998)
- Second Amendment Act 7 of 2010 (7 May 2010)
- Third Amendment Act 8 of 2014 (13 October 2014)

---

### PREAMBLE

Whereas recognition of the inherent dignity and of the equal and inalienable rights of all members of the human family is indispensable for freedom, justice and peace;

Whereas the said rights include the right of the individual to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, regardless of race, colour, ethnic origin, sex, religion, creed or social or economic status;

Whereas the said rights are most effectively maintained and protected in a democratic society, where the government is responsible to freely elected representatives of the people, operating under a sovereign constitution and a free and independent judiciary;

Whereas these rights have for so long been denied to the people of Namibia by colonialism, racism and apartheid;

Whereas we the people of Namibia — have finally emerged victorious in our struggle against colonialism, racism and apartheid; are determined to adopt a Constitution which expresses for ourselves and our children our resolve to cherish and to protect the gains of our long struggle; desire to promote amongst all of us the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Namibian nation among and in association with the nations of the world; will strive to achieve national reconciliation and to foster peace, unity and a common loyalty to a single state; committed to these principles, have resolved to constitute the Republic of Namibia as a sovereign, secular, democratic and unitary State securing to all our citizens justice, liberty, equality and fraternity,

**Now therefore, we the people of Namibia accept and adopt this Constitution as the fundamental law of our Sovereign and Independent Republic.**

---

### CHAPTER 1 – THE REPUBLIC

**Article 1 – Establishment of the Republic of Namibia**
1. The Republic of Namibia is hereby established as a sovereign, secular, democratic and unitary State founded upon the principles of democracy, the rule of law and justice for all.
2. All power shall vest in the people of Namibia who shall exercise their sovereignty through the democratic institutions of the State.
3. The main organs of the State shall be the Executive, the Legislature and the Judiciary.
4. The national territory of Namibia shall consist of the whole of the territory recognised by the international community through the organs of the United Nations as Namibia, including the enclave, harbour and port of Walvis Bay, as well as the off-shore islands of Namibia, and its southern boundary shall extend to the middle of the Orange River.
5. Windhoek shall be the seat of central Government.
6. **This Constitution shall be the Supreme Law of Namibia.**

**Article 2 – National Symbols**  
Namibia shall have a National Flag (described in Schedule 6), a National Coat of Arms, a National Anthem and a National Seal (to be determined by Act of Parliament requiring a two-thirds majority).

**Article 3 – Language**  
The official language of Namibia shall be **English**. Parliament may permit use of other languages for legislative, administrative and judicial purposes in regions where other languages are spoken by a substantial component of the population.

---

### CHAPTER 2 – CITIZENSHIP

**Article 4 – Acquisition and Loss of Citizenship**

Citizens by **birth**: Persons born in Namibia before or after Independence whose parents were/are Namibian citizens or ordinarily resident in Namibia (with exceptions for diplomatic staff, career representatives, and illegal immigrants).

Citizens by **descent**: Those whose parents were/would have been Namibian citizens, subject to registration requirements.

Citizens by **marriage**: Those who in good faith marry a Namibian citizen and thereafter ordinarily reside in Namibia as spouse for not less than **10 years**, then apply.

Citizens by **registration**: Persons ordinarily resident at Independence for at least 5 continuous years who applied within 12 months of Independence, having renounced other citizenship.

Citizens by **naturalisation**: Must be ordinarily resident in Namibia for not less than **10 continuous years** at time of application, plus health/morality/security criteria.

Loss of citizenship: By voluntary renunciation; Parliament may legislate loss for persons who acquire other citizenship voluntarily, serve in foreign armed forces without permission, or take up permanent foreign residence for more than 2 years without permission — **except** those who are citizens by birth or descent.

---

### CHAPTER 3 – FUNDAMENTAL HUMAN RIGHTS AND FREEDOMS

**Article 5 – Protection of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms**  
Fundamental rights shall be respected and upheld by the Executive, Legislature, Judiciary and all organs of Government, and shall be **enforceable by the Courts**.

**Article 6 – Protection of Life**  
The right to life shall be respected and protected. **No law may prescribe death as a competent sentence.** No Court shall impose a death sentence. No executions shall take place in Namibia.

**Article 7 – Protection of Liberty**  
No persons shall be deprived of personal liberty except according to **procedures established by law**.

**Article 8 – Respect for Human Dignity**  
1. The dignity of all persons shall be **inviolable**.
2. No persons shall be subject to **torture or to cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment**.

**Article 9 – Slavery and Forced Labour**  
No persons shall be held in slavery or servitude. No persons shall be required to perform forced labour. Exceptions include court-ordered labour, labour of lawfully detained persons for hygiene, defence/police/correctional service duties, labour during public emergencies, and communal/civic obligations.

**Article 10 – Equality and Freedom from Discrimination**  
1. All persons shall be equal before the law.
2. No persons may be discriminated against on the grounds of **sex, race, colour, ethnic origin, religion, creed or social or economic status**.

**Article 11 – Arrest and Detention**  
1. No arbitrary arrest or detention.
2. Arrested persons must be informed promptly in a language they understand of the grounds for arrest.
3. Arrested persons must be brought before the nearest Magistrate or judicial officer **within 48 hours** (or as soon as reasonably possible), and may not be detained beyond that period without judicial authority.
4. Illegal immigrants detained in custody: may not be deported without Tribunal authority; have the right to consult legal practitioners of their choice.

**Article 12 – Fair Trial**  
1. All persons shall be entitled to a **fair and public hearing** by an independent, impartial and competent Court or Tribunal:
   - Trial within a **reasonable time**, failing which the accused shall be released
   - Judgments in criminal cases given in public (except juvenile/morality cases)
   - **Presumption of innocence** until proven guilty
   - **Adequate time and facilities** for preparation of defence
   - Right to be defended by a **legal practitioner of their choice**
   - No compulsion to testify against oneself or spouse
   - Evidence obtained in violation of Article 8(2)(b) (torture, cruel treatment) is inadmissible
2. **Double jeopardy** protection — no person shall be tried again for an offence for which they have already been convicted or acquitted.
3. **Non-retroactivity** — no person shall be convicted for an act which was not criminal at the time it was committed; nor shall a heavier penalty be imposed than that applicable at the time of the offence.

**Article 13 – Privacy**  
No interference with privacy of homes, correspondence or communications except as is in accordance with law and **necessary in a democratic society** in the interests of national security, public safety, economic well-being, health, morals, prevention of disorder/crime, or protection of others' rights. Searches only justified when authorised by a competent judicial officer, or where delay would prejudice the objects of the search.

**Article 14 – Family**  
1. Men and women of full age have the right to marry without limitation due to race, colour, ethnic origin, nationality, religion, creed or social/economic status. **Equal rights** in marriage, during marriage, and at dissolution.
2. Marriage only with free and full consent of intending spouses.
3. The family is the natural and fundamental group unit of society.

**Article 15 – Children's Rights**  
1. Children have the right from birth to a name, to acquire a nationality, and as far as possible to know and be cared for by their parents.
2. Children are entitled to be protected from economic exploitation. Children for these purposes are persons under **16 years**.
3. No children under **14 years** shall be employed in any factory or mine.
5. No law authorising preventive detention shall permit children under **16 years** to be detained.

**Article 16 – Property**  
1. All persons have the right to acquire, own and dispose of all forms of immovable and movable property, individually or in association with others, and to bequeath their property. Parliament may regulate acquisition of property by non-citizens.
2. Expropriation only in the **public interest** subject to payment of **just compensation**, in accordance with requirements and procedures determined by Act of Parliament.

**Article 17 – Political Activity**  
1. All citizens have the right to participate in peaceful political activity, to form and join political parties, and to participate in the conduct of public affairs.
2. Every citizen aged **18 years** has the right to vote; aged **21 years** to be elected to public office.

**Article 18 – Administrative Justice**  
Administrative bodies and officials shall act **fairly and reasonably** and comply with common law and relevant legislation. Aggrieved persons have the right to seek redress before a competent Court or Tribunal.

**Article 19 – Culture**  
Every person is entitled to enjoy, practise, profess, maintain and promote any culture, language, tradition or religion, subject to the Constitution and provided it does not impinge on the rights of others or the national interest.

**Article 20 – Education**  
1. All persons have the right to education.
2. **Primary education is compulsory.** The State shall provide free primary education at State schools.
3. Children shall not leave school before completing primary education or reaching age 16, whichever is sooner.
4. Private schools, colleges and tertiary institutions may be established and maintained at own expense, provided they are registered, maintain adequate standards, and impose no racial/colour/creed restrictions.

**Article 21 – Fundamental Freedoms**  
All persons shall have the right to:
- (a) **Freedom of speech and expression**, including freedom of the press and other media
- (b) **Freedom of thought, conscience and belief**, including academic freedom
- (c) **Freedom to practise any religion**
- (d) **Peaceful assembly** without arms
- (e) **Freedom of association**, including to form and join trade unions and political parties
- (f) Withhold labour without criminal penalty
- (g) **Move freely** throughout Namibia
- (h) **Reside and settle** in any part of Namibia
- (i) **Leave and return** to Namibia
- (j) **Practise any profession** or carry on any occupation, trade or business

These freedoms may be limited by law only in so far as such limitations are reasonable, necessary in a democratic society, and required in the interests of sovereignty/integrity, national security, public order, decency or morality, or in relation to contempt of court, defamation or incitement to an offence.

**Article 22 – Limitation upon Fundamental Rights**  
Any law limiting fundamental rights shall: be of general application; not negate the essential content; not target a particular individual; specify the ascertainable extent of the limitation; and identify the Article(s) on which authority to limit is claimed.

**Article 23 – Apartheid and Affirmative Action**  
1. The practice of racial discrimination and apartheid is prohibited and may be rendered criminally punishable by Parliament.
2. Parliament may enact legislation for the **advancement of historically disadvantaged persons** (affirmative action) to redress social, economic or educational imbalances, or to achieve balanced structuring of the public service, defence force, police force, and correctional service.
3. Women in Namibia who have traditionally suffered special discrimination may be specifically targeted by such legislation.

**Article 24 – Derogation**  
Derogation from rights is permitted during state of national defence or emergency under Article 26, subject to safeguards including written statement within 5 days, Gazette notification within 14 days, and review by Advisory Board within 1 month. No derogation from Articles 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 18, 19 and 21(1)(a)(b)(c)(e), or from the right of access to legal practitioners or Courts.

**Article 25 – Enforcement of Fundamental Rights**  
1. Parliament shall not make any law, and the Executive shall not take any action, that abolishes or abridges fundamental rights. Contravening laws or actions are invalid.
2. **Aggrieved persons** may approach a competent Court to enforce or protect their rights, or the Ombudsman for legal assistance.
3. Courts may make all orders necessary to secure enjoyment of rights, including **monetary compensation**.

### CHAPTER 4 – PUBLIC EMERGENCY, STATE OF NATIONAL DEFENCE AND MARTIAL LAW

**Article 26 – State of Emergency, State of National Defence and Martial Law**  
1. The President may by Proclamation in the Gazette declare a state of emergency during national disaster, state of national defence, or public emergency threatening the life of the nation or constitutional order.
2. Declaration ceases unless approved by **two-thirds majority** of National Assembly within 7 days (if Assembly sitting) or 30 days otherwise.
3. When approved, continues for up to **6 months**, extendable by two-thirds majority for further 6-month periods.
4. National Assembly may revoke at any time.
5. During emergency, President may make regulations including suspension of laws and fundamental rights (subject to Article 24). Detention without trial provisions require an Advisory Board of at least 3 Judges.
7. Martial law may only be proclaimed during state of national defence against another country or civil war; must be approved by two-thirds majority of National Assembly within a reasonable time.

---

### CHAPTER 5 – THE PRESIDENT

**Article 27 – Head of State and Government**  
The President is Head of State, Head of Government, and **Commander-in-Chief** of the Defence Force. Executive power vests in the President and Cabinet.

**Article 28 – Election of the President and appointment of the Vice-President**  
- Elected by **direct, universal and equal suffrage**
- Must receive more than **50% of valid votes cast**; if no candidate does, a second ballot is held between the two top candidates
- Eligible: Namibian citizen by birth or descent, over **35 years** of age, eligible as National Assembly member
- The **Vice-President** is appointed by the President from elected members of the National Assembly

**Article 29 – Term of Office**  
- Term: **5 years**
- A person shall hold office as President for not more than **two terms**
- Removal requires two-thirds majority of National Assembly confirmed by two-thirds majority of National Council on grounds of constitutional violation, serious legal violation, or gross misconduct

**Article 30 – Oath or Affirmation of the President**  
Administered by the Chief Justice, Deputy-Chief Justice, or a designated Judge. Oath to uphold and defend the Constitution, faithfully obey and administer laws, protect independence and sovereignty, and ensure justice for all.

**Article 31 – Immunity from Civil and Criminal Proceedings**  
- No civil proceedings against the President for acts done in official capacity while in office
- No criminal charges against a sitting President for acts during tenure
- After leaving office: no civil action for official acts; criminal/civil proceedings for personal acts require parliamentary resolution in the public interest

**Article 32 – Functions, Powers and Duties**  
Key presidential powers include: dissolving the National Assembly; declaring states of emergency/martial law; appointing Vice-President, Prime Minister, Ministers, Attorney-General, Judges (on JSC recommendation), Chief of Defence Force, Inspector-General of Police (on Security Commission recommendation); pardoning offenders; negotiating and signing international agreements; appointing not more than **8 non-voting members** of the National Assembly by virtue of special expertise.

**Article 34 – Succession**  
If the office of President becomes vacant: Vice-President acts first; then Prime Minister; then Deputy-Prime Minister; then a Cabinet member appointed by the President.

---

### CHAPTER 6 – THE CABINET

**Article 35 – Composition**  
Cabinet consists of the President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Deputy-Prime Minister, and Ministers from members of the National Assembly.

**Article 36 – Functions of the Prime Minister**  
Leader of Government business in Parliament; co-ordinates work of Cabinet as head of administration.

**Article 39 – Vote of No Confidence**  
President obliged to terminate appointment of any Cabinet member if National Assembly by majority resolves it has no confidence in that member.

**Article 40 – Duties and Functions of Cabinet**  
Includes directing and co-ordinating Ministries, initiating bills, formulating the budget, reporting to Parliament on foreign policy and trade, advising on national defence, and combating apartheid, tribalism and colonialism.

**Article 41 – Ministerial Accountability**  
Ministers are accountable individually for their own Ministries and collectively for the work of Cabinet, to both the President and Parliament.

---

### CHAPTER 7 – THE NATIONAL ASSEMBLY

**Article 44 – Legislative Power**  
The **legislative power of Namibia** is vested in the National Assembly.

**Article 46 – Composition**  
- **96 elected members** (by proportional representation/party list system, Schedule 4)
- Up to **8 appointed members** (by President, no voting rights) by virtue of special expertise

**Article 47 – Disqualification of Members**  
Persons disqualified if convicted of an offence with sentence of death or more than 12 months' imprisonment without option of fine (imprisonment expired less than 10 years before election); unrehabilitated insolvents; persons of unsound mind; remunerated public servants; members of National Council, Regional Councils or Local Authorities.

**Article 48 – Vacation of Seats**  
Seats vacated if: qualifications cease; party informs Speaker membership ended; resignation; removal for good cause; absence for 10 consecutive sitting days without special leave.

**Article 49 – Elections**  
Party lists, proportional representation (Schedule 4).

**Article 50 – Duration**  
National Assembly continues for maximum **5 years**; may be dissolved earlier by Presidential Proclamation.

**Article 56 – Assent to Bills**  
Bills require Presidential assent. Bills passed by two-thirds majority of National Assembly and confirmed by National Council: President **obliged** to assent. Bills passed by simple majority: President may withhold assent and communicate dissent to Speaker; National Assembly may reconsider.

**Article 63 – Functions and Powers**  
National Assembly may make and repeal laws for peace, order and good government; approve budgets; provide for revenue and taxation; ratify international agreements; hold referenda on matters of national concern.

**Article 66 – Customary and Common Law**  
Both customary law and common law in force on date of Independence remain valid unless inconsistent with the Constitution or statutory law. Parliament may repeal or modify.

**Article 67 – Requisite Majorities**  
Simple majority of votes cast is sufficient for passage of any bill or resolution, unless otherwise specified.

---

### CHAPTER 8 – THE NATIONAL COUNCIL

**Article 68 – Establishment**  
There shall be a National Council with the powers and functions set out in the Constitution.

**Article 69 – Composition**  
**3 members from each region** (14 regions = 42 members total), elected by each Regional Council from amongst their members.

**Article 70 – Term of Office**  
Members hold seats for **5 years** from date of election.

**Article 74 – Powers and Functions**  
- Considers all bills passed by the National Assembly
- Investigates and reports on subordinate legislation, reports and documents referred to it
- Recommends legislation on matters of regional concern
- May establish committees

**Article 75 – Review of Legislation**  
All bills passed by National Assembly referred to National Council. If National Council: confirms — bill goes to President; recommends amendments — bill referred back to National Assembly; objects to principle (two-thirds majority) — National Assembly must reconsider; if National Assembly reaffirms by two-thirds majority, bill proceeds. National Council has **30 days** for tax/appropriation bills and **3 months** for other bills, failing which deemed to have confirmed.

---

### CHAPTER 9 – THE ADMINISTRATION OF JUSTICE

**Article 78 – The Judiciary**  
Judicial power vested in Courts: Supreme Court, High Court, Lower Courts. Courts are **independent** and subject only to the Constitution and the law. No Cabinet member, legislator, or other person may interfere with Judges in the exercise of their judicial functions.

**Article 79 – The Supreme Court**  
Consists of Chief Justice, Deputy-Chief Justice, and additional Judges. Hears appeals from the High Court. Quorum: 3 Judges. Also handles matters referred by Attorney-General.

**Article 80 – The High Court**  
Consists of Judge-President (who is also the Deputy-Chief Justice), Deputy Judges-President, and additional Judges. Has **original jurisdiction** over all civil disputes and criminal prosecutions, including constitutional matters. Also hears appeals from Lower Courts.

**Article 81 – Binding Nature of Supreme Court Decisions**  
Supreme Court decisions are **binding on all other Courts** and all persons in Namibia, unless reversed by the Supreme Court itself or contradicted by an Act of Parliament lawfully enacted.

**Article 82 – Appointment of Judges**  
All Judges appointed by President on recommendation of the **Judicial Service Commission**. Judges hold office until age **65**, extendable to **70**. Non-Namibian citizen Judges appointed under fixed term contracts.

**Article 83 – Lower Courts (Magistrates' Courts)**  
Established by Act of Parliament. Presided over by Magistrates. A **Magistrates Commission** is responsible for transfer, discipline, removal, remuneration and conditions of service of Magistrates (established by the Magistrates Act 3 of 2003).

**Article 84 – Removal of Judges from Office**  
Judges may only be removed on grounds of mental incapacity or gross misconduct, following investigation by a tribunal established by the Judicial Service Commission.

**Article 85 – The Judicial Service Commission**  
Consists of: Chief Justice, Deputy-Chief Justice, Attorney-General, and two members of the legal profession nominated by professional organisations.

**Article 86 – The Attorney-General**  
Appointed by the President.

**Article 87 – Powers and Functions of the Attorney-General**  
- Final responsibility for the office of the Prosecutor-General
- Principal legal adviser to the President and Government
- Takes action necessary to protect and uphold the Constitution

**Article 88 – The Prosecutor-General**  
Appointed by President on recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission. Must possess legal qualifications to practise in all Courts of Namibia. Powers: prosecute in the name of the Republic in criminal proceedings; prosecute and defend criminal appeals; delegate authority to conduct criminal proceedings.

---

### CHAPTER 10 – THE OMBUDSMAN

**Article 89 – Establishment and Independence**  
The Ombudsman is independent and subject only to the Constitution and the law. Must be a Judge or possess legal qualifications to practise in all Courts of Namibia.

**Article 90 – Appointment**  
Appointed by President on recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission. Holds office until age 65 (extendable to 70).

**Article 91 – Functions**  
- Investigate complaints about violations of fundamental rights and freedoms
- Investigate complaints about abuse of power, unfair treatment, manifest injustice, unlawful or oppressive conduct by Government officials
- Investigate complaints about the functioning of the Public Service Commission, defence force, police force, correctional service regarding balanced structuring and equal access
- Investigate complaints about environmental degradation and failure to protect Namibia's natural beauty
- Investigate practices by private persons/enterprises alleging violations of constitutional rights
- Investigate misappropriation of public monies
- Report annually to the National Assembly

---

### CHAPTER 10A – ANTI-CORRUPTION MEASURES

**Article 94A – Anti-Corruption Commission**  
The State shall put in place administrative and legislative measures to prevent and combat corruption. There is an **Anti-Corruption Commission** (established under the Anti-Corruption Act 8 of 2003), which is independent and impartial. It consists of a Director-General, Deputy Director-General and staff. The Director-General and Deputy Director-General are appointed by the National Assembly on Presidential nomination, for terms of **5 years**.

---

### CHAPTER 10B – ELECTORAL COMMISSION OF NAMIBIA

**Article 94B – Electoral Commission of Namibia**  
The **exclusive body** to direct, supervise, manage and control elections and referenda. Independent, transparent and impartial. Consists of **5 Commissioners** (including Chairperson) appointed by President with approval of National Assembly, for **5-year terms** (maximum **2 terms**). The Chief Electoral and Referenda Officer is the depository of electoral records.

---

### CHAPTER 11 – PRINCIPLES OF STATE POLICY

**Article 95 – Promotion of the Welfare of the People**  
State policy principles include:
- (a) Legislation ensuring equality of opportunity for women; maternity benefits
- (b) Legislation ensuring health and strength of workers not abused; children not in unsuitable vocations
- (c) Encouragement of independent trade unions
- (d) Membership of the ILO and adherence to ILO Conventions
- (e) Fair and reasonable access to public facilities and services
- (f) Regular adequate pensions for senior citizens
- (g) Social benefits for the unemployed, incapacitated, indigent and disadvantaged
- (h) Free legal aid in defined cases
- (i) Living wages adequate for decent standard of living
- (j) Consistent planning to raise nutrition and public health standards
- (k) Encouragement of the population to influence government policy through debate
- (l) Maintenance of ecosystems, biological diversity, and sustainable use of living natural resources; no dumping of foreign nuclear and toxic waste on Namibian territory

**Note:** These principles are **not legally enforceable** by Courts on their own but guide the Government and Courts in interpretation (Article 101).

**Article 96 – Foreign Relations**  
Namibia shall: adopt a policy of non-alignment; promote international co-operation, peace and security; create mutually beneficial international relations; foster respect for international law; encourage peaceful settlement of international disputes.

**Article 97 – Asylum**  
Namibia shall grant asylum where reasonable to persons who reasonably fear persecution on grounds of political beliefs, race, religion or membership of a particular social group.

**Article 98 – Principles of Economic Order**  
The economic order shall be based on a **mixed economy** aiming at economic growth, prosperity and human dignity. Forms of ownership include: public, private, joint public-private, co-operative, co-ownership, small-scale family.

**Article 100 – Sovereign Ownership of Natural Resources**  
Land, water and natural resources (below and above the surface, continental shelf, territorial waters and exclusive economic zone) belong to the **State** if not otherwise lawfully owned.

---

### CHAPTER 12 – REGIONAL AND LOCAL GOVERNMENT

**Article 102 – Structures of Regional and Local Government**  
Namibia is divided into regional and local units. Boundaries are geographical only, with no reference to race, colour or ethnic origin. Every organ of regional/local government has a freely elected Council as principal governing body. A **Council of Traditional Leaders** advises the President on communal land control and other matters.

**Article 103 – Regional Councils**  
Namibia is divided into **14 regions**. A Regional Council is established for every region.

**Article 104 – Boundaries Delimitation and Demarcation Commission**  
Delimits and demarcates Namibia's boundaries; recommends boundaries of regions, constituencies and local authorities. Consists of a full-time Chairperson and part-time Commissioners appointed by President with approval of National Assembly.

**Article 106 – Regional Council Elections**  
Each region divided into constituencies (minimum 6, maximum 12 per region). Each constituency elects one member to the Regional Council. Elections by secret ballot; candidate receiving most votes wins. All Regional Council elections held on the same day.

**Article 108 – Powers of Regional Councils**  
- Elect members to the National Council
- Exercise executive powers assigned by Parliament/President
- Raise revenue or share in central government revenue
- Make by-laws and regulations as determined by Act of Parliament

**Article 111 – Local Authorities**  
Boundaries, elections, revenue-raising, Councillor remuneration, and all matters relating to Local Authorities determined by Act of Parliament. Voters must have been resident within the Local Authority jurisdiction for not less than **1 year** before election and be qualified to vote in National Assembly elections.

---

### CHAPTER 13 – THE PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION

**Article 112 – Establishment**  
Advises the President on public service matters. Independent and impartial. Consists of a Chairperson and 3–6 members nominated by President and appointed by National Assembly. Members serve for **5 years**.

**Article 113 – Functions**  
Advises on appointments, disciplinary control, remuneration, and all other public service matters. May advise President on identity and suitability of persons to be appointed to constitutional offices.

---

### CHAPTER 14 – THE SECURITY COMMISSION

**Article 114 – Establishment and Functions**  
Makes recommendations on appointment of Chief of Defence Force, Inspector-General of Police, and Commissioner-General of Correctional Service. Consists of: Chairperson of Public Service Commission, Chief of Defence Force, Inspector-General of Police, Head of Intelligence Service, Commissioner-General of Correctional Service, and 2 National Assembly members appointed by President.

---

### CHAPTER 15 – THE DEFENCE FORCE, POLICE FORCE, INTELLIGENCE SERVICE AND CORRECTIONAL SERVICE

**Article 115 – Namibian Defence Force**  
Established by Act of Parliament to defend Namibia's territory and national interests. President is Commander-in-Chief.

**Article 118 – Namibian Police Force**  
Established by Act of Parliament to secure internal security and maintain law and order.

**Article 120A – Intelligence Service**  
Established by Act of Parliament (Namibia Central Intelligence Service Act 10 of 1997). Head appointed by President; serves at pleasure of President; principal adviser on national security and intelligence.

**Article 121 – Correctional Service**  
Established by Act of Parliament with prescribed powers, duties and procedures.

---

### CHAPTER 16 – FINANCE

**Article 125 – The State Revenue Fund**  
All income accruing to the central Government shall be deposited in the State Revenue Fund. No money shall be withdrawn except in accordance with an Act of Parliament.

**Article 126 – Appropriations**  
The Minister responsible for finance shall at least once every year present estimates of revenue, expenditure and income (national budget) to the National Assembly.

**Article 127 – The Auditor-General**  
Appointed by President on recommendation of Public Service Commission with approval of National Assembly. Holds office for **5 years**. Audits the State Revenue Fund and reports annually to the National Assembly. Removed only if two-thirds majority of National Assembly votes for removal on grounds of mental incapacity or gross misconduct.

---

### CHAPTER 17 – CENTRAL BANK AND NATIONAL PLANNING COMMISSION

**Article 128 – The Central Bank (Bank of Namibia)**  
Principal instrument to control money supply, currency and banking institutions. Governing Board consists of Governor, Deputy-Governors, and other members, all appointed by the President.

**Article 129 – National Planning Commission**  
Established in the Presidency to plan the priorities and direction of national development. Director-General of Planning is head and principal adviser to President on economic planning.

---

### CHAPTER 19 – AMENDMENT OF THE CONSTITUTION

**Article 131 – Entrenchment of Fundamental Rights**  
No repeal or amendment of Chapter 3 provisions that diminishes fundamental rights is permissible. Any such amendment is void.

**Article 132 – Repeal and Amendment**  
Requires: **(a)** two-thirds of all National Assembly members; AND **(b)** two-thirds of all National Council members. If National Assembly achieves two-thirds but National Council does not, President may call a **national referendum**, where approval requires two-thirds of all votes cast.

---

### CHAPTER 20 – THE LAW IN FORCE AND TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS

**Article 140 – The Law in Force at Date of Independence**  
All laws in force immediately before Independence remain in force until repealed, amended, or declared unconstitutional. References to South African Government officers deemed to refer to corresponding Namibian officers.

**Article 143 – Existing International Agreements**  
All existing international agreements binding upon Namibia remain in force unless the National Assembly otherwise decides.

---

### CHAPTER 21 – FINAL PROVISIONS

**Article 144 – International Law**  
Unless otherwise provided by the Constitution or Act of Parliament, the **general rules of public international law and international agreements binding upon Namibia form part of the law of Namibia**.

**Article 148 – Short Title**  
This Constitution shall be called the **Namibian Constitution**.

---

### SCHEDULE 1 – Oath/Affirmation of Judges
"I...do hereby swear/solemnly affirm that as a Judge of the Republic of Namibia I will defend and uphold the Constitution of the Republic of Namibia as the Supreme Law and will fearlessly administer justice to all persons without favour or prejudice and in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Namibia."

### SCHEDULE 4 – Election of Members of the National Assembly
Proportional representation system: Total valid votes divided by 96 (seats) = quota per seat. Each party's total votes divided by quota = number of seats. Remaining/surplus seats allocated by highest surplus fraction. If tie, allocated by lot.
